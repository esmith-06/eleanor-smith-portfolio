Entrepreneurship

Before I ever took a marketing class, I was running a business. At twelve years old, I started Sweet E's Bakery out of my kitchen in Lake Mills, Iowa, baking and decorating custom cakes and cupcakes for people in my community. I taught myself every part of the craft, relying on creativity and a lot of trial and error to master basic baking techniques and advanced cake decorating. I ran Sweet E's Bakery for six years, from age twelve to eighteen, alongside school, sports, and eventually a part-time job, and it became my first real lesson in entrepreneurship.

Running a small business that young taught me entrepreneurship lessons that no classroom could fully replicate. Every order meant real customers, real deadlines, and real money, so I learned quickly that quality, consistency, and branding mattered more than anything else. I dedicated multiple hours to every single order to make sure each cake or batch of cupcakes reflected the brand I was trying to build. Looking back, running that business was really a crash course in small-business marketing and branding, even if I didn't have those words for it at the time.

Marketing was never something I formally studied for Sweet E's Bakery; it was something I had to figure out on the fly. I advertised the business across multiple social media platforms to reach new customers in my area, creating simple marketing content, photos and short videos of finished orders, and relying on word of mouth to build a small but loyal customer base. I also sold my products in person at weekly local farmers markets, which forced me to develop strong communication and sales skills, since good branding, good marketing, and a good product only sell themselves so far.

Balancing Sweet E's Bakery with academics and another part-time job also meant learning real time management long before I had a planner or a calendar app to help. Looking back, Sweet E's Bakery is where my interest in marketing actually started. Branding, creativity, and marketing were not abstract concepts to me; they were the difference between a slow month and a full order book. That entrepreneurial foundation is a big part of why I chose to study marketing at Iowa State, and it still shapes how I think about building a brand people actually want to come back to.
